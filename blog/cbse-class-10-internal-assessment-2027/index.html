CBSE Class 10 Internal Assessment 2027: How the 20 Marks Are Earned, Subject by Subject

Most families talk about the 80-mark board paper and treat the other 20 marks as a formality that the school takes care of. They are not a formality. Internal assessment is a fifth of every major subject, it is earned slowly over the year through tests, projects and portfolios, and unlike a three-hour paper it is largely in your child's control. It is also the part parents hear least about until a deadline is already close. Here is how CBSE's own 2026-27 curriculum says the 20 marks work, subject by subject, and how to protect them.

The short answer

In each major subject, a Class 10 student's result combines 80 marks from the CBSE board paper and 20 marks of internal assessment, which the school runs during the year. The 20 marks are built from four components of equal weight (periodic tests, multiple assessment, a portfolio and subject enrichment), so each is worth about 5 marks. Every internal mark counts exactly as much as a theory mark, and they are earned over months rather than in three hours. Ask your school for the rubrics and deadlines now, not in January.

What internal assessment is, and why it is not a formality

CBSE's 2026-27 curriculum states that the assessment structure for Classes 9 and 10 has two mandatory components: the Board examination for Class 10 (80 marks for major subjects), and Internal Assessment (20 marks for major subjects), conducted by schools. The final result combines both, and students must appear in and complete both.

The same document adds that a student must secure a minimum of 33% marks "separately in external and internal assessments", calculated on the combined total, as per the prescribed scheme. Coaching websites describe the exact calculation differently, and the details sit in CBSE's examination bye-laws, so confirm the precise rule with your school. The safe takeaway for a family is simple: internal marks are a separate component that has to be completed and cleared. They are not a bonus that can be ignored.

The four components, in plain language

CBSE describes four internal assessment components, each carrying 25% of the internal weightage. For a 20-mark subject, that is about 5 marks each.

  • Periodic assessment. Three periodic assessments are held in each subject during the year, and the average of the best two is taken. CBSE also says at least one of them must be competency-based, with case analysis, data interpretation or problem-solving. This is why a student who misses or does poorly in one test is not ruined by it, but should not rely on that.
  • Multiple assessment. A mix of ways to show learning, such as observation, oral presentations, discussions, quizzes, experiments, projects, fieldwork and concept maps. CBSE says schools should develop simple, transparent rubrics and share them with students in advance.
  • Portfolio. A curated collection of the student's work that shows progress over time: written work, projects, reflections, experimental records. CBSE stresses quality of engagement and growth rather than the volume of material, and says rubrics must be shared in advance.
  • Subject enrichment activities. Inquiry and application work that goes beyond the textbook: investigations and modelling in Maths and Science, case studies and civic inquiry in Social Science, and listening and speaking in languages.

One more detail from the curriculum is worth knowing: the best performance across the prescribed components is considered when the school awards the final internal assessment credit, in line with the weightage rules. In practice, effort that starts early gives a student more than one chance to show what they can do.

How the 20 marks are split, subject by subject

CBSE publishes the split inside each subject's curriculum. These are the Class 10 breakdowns for 2026-27.

SubjectHow the 20 internal marks are split
MathematicsPen-paper test and multiple assessment: 10 marks (5 + 5). Portfolio: 5 marks. Lab practical: 5 marks, from the lab activities in the prescribed books.
SciencePeriodic assessment: 5. Multiple assessment: 5. Portfolio: 5. Subject enrichment (practical work): 5.
Social SciencePeriodic assessment (pen-paper test): 5. Multiple assessment: 5. Subject enrichment, an interdisciplinary project on consumer rights, social issues or sustainable development: 5. Portfolio: 5.
EnglishListening and speaking carry 5 marks as part of subject enrichment. For the rest of the 20 marks, CBSE's English curriculum refers to a separate circular (Acad-11/2019), so ask your school for the breakdown it follows.

Source: the subject curricula in CBSE's 2026-27 Class 10 curriculum. Schools decide the specific tasks, dates and rubrics.

Notice what the table shows. In Maths, Science and Social Science, each subject has a project or practical element, a portfolio and at least one test. That means internal assessment is spread across the year and across several skills. A student who is strong at written exams but weak at organising projects, or the reverse, will see it here.

What each internal mark is actually worth

Every internal mark is worth exactly the same as a theory mark, which is easiest to see when you work backwards from a target. This table shows the theory marks, out of 80, a student needs to reach 80 or 90 out of 100 in a subject, depending on their internal score.

Simple arithmetic: target marks minus internal marks. Percentages show what the theory score represents out of 80.

A student who loses six internal marks through a missed project or an unfinished portfolio has to find six extra marks in a three-hour exam, usually in the hardest questions. Across five subjects, careless internal marks can quietly add up to a 20 to 30 mark gap that no amount of last-minute revision fully closes. Eight ways to protect internal marks

  1. Get the calendar and the rubrics early. Ask each subject teacher for the dates of the three periodic assessments, the project deadline and the portfolio format. CBSE says rubrics should be shared with students in advance, so it is reasonable to ask for them.
  2. Treat every periodic test as a mini board exam. The best two of three are averaged. Revise for each one properly, and remember that at least one will be competency-based, so practise unfamiliar-style questions. Our guide to CBSE competency-based questions explains what those look like.
  3. Start the portfolio in week one, not week forty. Keep a folder. Add the best piece of work from each chapter, a short reflection on what was learned and any lab or project record. A curated, tidy portfolio scores better than a last-minute pile.
  4. Break the project into stages. Choose the topic, collect sources, draft, revise, present. Put one date in the calendar for each stage. Projects rushed over a weekend usually look it.
  5. Keep science and maths practical records up to date. Write up each experiment or lab activity soon after doing it. Records written months later from memory tend to lose marks.
  6. Practise speaking and listening in English. Five marks come from it. Reading aloud, short talks to the family and recording a one-minute summary on a phone are all good practice.
  7. Turn up and submit. Missing a test, a viva or a submission date is the most avoidable way to lose marks. If your child is ill, tell the school immediately and ask how the work can be completed.
  8. Let your child do the work. Parent-made projects are usually obvious to teachers and, more importantly, they remove the learning that the marks are meant to reward. Help with planning and feedback, not with writing or building.

Questions to ask your child's school

  • When are the three periodic assessments, and how will the best two be taken?
  • What are the rubrics for the portfolio and the multiple assessment tasks?
  • What is the project topic and the deadline for each stage?
  • How are the English listening and speaking marks assessed, and when?
  • How do we see the internal marks as the year goes on, so there are no surprises?
  • If a student misses a task because of illness, what is the process?

Questions like these fit well into a parent-teacher meeting, and they show your child that internal assessment is taken seriously at home too.

It starts in Class 9

CBSE's curriculum says internal assessment is conducted by schools for both Class 9 and Class 10, with an 80-mark annual examination and 20 marks of internal assessment in Class 9 as well. That makes Class 9 the natural year to build the habits: a tidy portfolio, projects done in stages and a calm approach to periodic tests. Where tuition fits

Internal assessment belongs to the school and to the student, and a tutor should never do the work for them. What good support can do is help with the parts that benefit from guidance: preparing for periodic tests, including the competency-based one, planning a project into manageable steps, and giving English speaking practice that builds confidence.

Frequently asked questions

What is internal assessment in CBSE Class 10?

It is the part of the Class 10 result that the school assesses during the year. For major subjects it is worth 20 marks, and the board paper is worth 80. The result combines both. Internal assessment is built from periodic tests, multiple assessment such as quizzes and projects, a portfolio, and subject enrichment activities.

Out of how many marks is internal assessment in Class 10?

For major subjects, internal assessment is out of 20, and the Board examination is out of 80, which makes 100 per subject. Some subjects have different marks, as specified in their own curriculum.

How are the 20 internal marks split in Maths and Science?

In Maths, CBSE's 2026-27 curriculum gives 10 marks to a pen-paper test and multiple assessment (5 + 5), 5 to a portfolio and 5 to lab practical work. In Science, it is 5 for periodic assessment, 5 for multiple assessment, 5 for a portfolio and 5 for subject enrichment, which includes practical work.

What happens if my child misses a periodic test?

CBSE's curriculum says three periodic assessments are held in each subject and the average of the best two is taken, so one weak or missed test does not automatically reduce the marks. How a missed test is handled in practice depends on your school's policy, so tell the school early and ask for the process.

Can internal assessment marks be improved?

Yes, during the year. The curriculum says the best performance across the prescribed components is considered for the final internal credit, and periodic assessment uses the best two of three tests. Once the school submits the final marks the year's work is complete, which is why starting early matters.

Does tuition help with internal assessment?

It can help with preparing for periodic tests and with planning projects and speaking practice, but the work must be the student's own. The most useful support is organised guidance and practice, never doing the project or portfolio for the child.
