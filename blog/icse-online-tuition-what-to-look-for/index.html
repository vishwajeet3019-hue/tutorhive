ICSE Online Tuition: What Parents Should Look For When Choosing a Tutor

Most online tuition advice is written with CBSE in mind, and for good reason: CBSE schools all follow the same NCERT textbooks, so a tutor can prepare for them in advance. If your child studies in an ICSE school, the picture is different, and the usual advice does not always fit. The practical question for an ICSE parent is not "does this tutor know the board?" but "will this tutor work from my child's own books, at my child's school's pace, and help with the skills that school tests?" Here is how to find out, including a simple way to test a tutor in the first trial class.

The short answer

For an ICSE child, the best tutor is one who works from your child's own school textbooks and notes, follows the school's pace and tests, and is strong in English and written answers. CBSE schools follow NCERT's books, but ICSE schools generally choose their own, so ask what your child actually uses. In the trial class, bring a textbook chapter and a recent corrected test, and watch whether the tutor explains clearly, makes your child do the work and spots where marks were lost.

What is different when the school is ICSE

In CBSE schools, the textbook list is shared nationally: NCERT publishes the books, and every school follows them. A tutor can learn the Class 6 or Class 8 books once and use them for every student.

ICSE is run by the Council for the Indian School Certificate Examinations (CISCE), which sets the syllabus. In general, ICSE schools choose their own textbooks and teaching plans within that syllabus, so the books and the order of chapters can differ from one ICSE school to another. This is the single biggest reason ICSE tuition needs a slightly different approach: the tutor has to start from your child's school, not from a standard book list. Syllabus details and board rules are set by CISCE, so check cisce.org or your school for the exact current position.

A simple way to test a tutor in the trial class

A good trial tells you far more than a brochure. Here is how to use it.

  1. Bring your child's own book and a corrected test. Ask the tutor to work with a chapter your child finds hard, not a generic example.
  2. Watch who does the thinking. The tutor should ask questions, wait for your child to answer and let them attempt a problem before helping. If the tutor talks for most of the session, that is a lecture, not tuition.
  3. Ask the tutor to look at the corrected test. A good tutor will quickly say where marks were lost: understanding, careless slips, or how the answer was written.
  4. Ask for one question in the style of your school. This shows whether the tutor can follow what your school tests.
  5. Ask your child afterwards. Did they understand? Were they comfortable asking questions? Would they like another class?
  6. Ask how progress will be shared. A clear answer here is a good sign.

Why English deserves special attention

Whatever the subject, children learn and show what they know through language. A child who reads slowly, struggles to understand a question or writes unclearly will lose marks in science, social studies and even maths word problems. For ICSE families it is worth checking that the tutor can help with reading and writing, not only the content of the subject. Our guides on reading comprehension, English writing skills and reading habits give simple routines you can use at home alongside any class.

What to focus on in Classes 5 to 10

  • Classes 5 and 6: reading fluency, tables and fractions, and short written answers. Keep sessions short and interactive. See our guide on whether a Class 5 child needs tuition.
  • Classes 7 and 8: longer written answers, the first real depth in maths and science, and building the habit of asking questions. If marks start slipping, read why marks drop in Classes 6 to 8.
  • Classes 9 and 10: a heavier syllabus and board preparation. Choose a tutor with strong subject knowledge, and make sure the school's assessment plan is shared with them. Ask the school for the latest details from CISCE.

In every class, the basics of choosing well are the same as for any board. Our guide to choosing online tuition and the research summary in is online tuition effective explain what makes tutoring work: small groups, regular sessions and a clear focus on the child's gaps.

Frequently asked questions

Is online tuition for ICSE different from CBSE?

The principles of good tuition are the same: small groups, regular sessions and a focus on the child's gaps. The practical difference is that CBSE schools follow NCERT textbooks, while ICSE schools generally choose their own books, so an ICSE tutor needs to work from your child's school books and syllabus.

What should I share with an ICSE tutor before the trial class?

Share the textbook list, the school syllabus or term plan, a recent corrected test and any notes on where your child finds things hard. This lets the tutor prepare from the right place instead of a generic lesson.

How do I know a tutor is strong enough for ICSE English?

Ask them to explain a grammar point and a comprehension answer in the trial class, and to look at a piece of your child's writing. A good tutor can explain simply, give clear feedback and show how to improve the next answer.

At what class should ICSE students start tuition?

There is no fixed class. Tuition helps most when there is a specific gap or a persistent struggle. In Class 5 and 6 many children manage with home support, and tuition becomes more useful as the syllabus gets heavier. Use the signs in our guide to decide.

Where do I check the official ICSE rules?

The Council for the Indian School Certificate Examinations (CISCE) publishes the ICSE syllabus and regulations on cisce.org. Your child's school will also have the current details.
