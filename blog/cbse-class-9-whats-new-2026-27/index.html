What Is New in CBSE Class 9 for 2026-27, and What Parents Should Do About It

If your child is in Class 9 this year, you may have noticed that the textbooks look different from the ones their elder cousin used, that Maths has chapters nobody remembers from school, and that a third language has appeared on the timetable. You are not imagining it. Several things changed at once for the 2026-27 session, and the changes are easy to miss because they arrive through different documents: NCERT's new textbooks, CBSE's curriculum, and school circulars. This guide pulls them together in one place, explains what each change means for study at home, and gives you a short list of things to do this term.

The short answer

Class 9 in 2026-27 has new NCERT textbooks (Ganita Manjari for Maths, Exploration for Science, Kaveri for English), Maths chapters that earlier books did not have, a compulsory third language, and questions that test reasoning more than recall. The annual exam is set by your school, with 20 marks of internal assessment. Older guides and notes will not match, so check the books and the school's plan first, then build the habit of explaining answers, not just writing them.

What the new Maths book asks of your child

NCERT describes Ganita Manjari as a transition from middle stage to secondary mathematics: while staying connected to what students already know, it brings more conceptual depth, formal reasoning and the beginnings of proof. A few features are worth knowing.

  • It starts with coordinates and number systems, then builds algebra through polynomials, sequences and progressions. A child who is comfortable plotting points and working with fractions and decimals has a head start.
  • Several chapters are about why, not just how. Chapter 9 of Part II is about the difference between proving a statement and proving its converse. Chapter 11 introduces algorithms, using addition and the greatest common divisor, and ends with Euclid's remainder-based method. Chapter 10 covers weighted averages and compares stacked and 100% stacked bar charts.
  • A low-threshold, high-ceiling approach. NCERT says every chapter is meant to be accessible to all learners while offering harder problems for those who want more. The harder problems are marked with an asterisk and are not examined.
  • Explaining is part of the work. NCERT says selected questions challenge students to explain their reasoning and construct arguments.

In practice, this means that a child who memorises steps will find Class 9 Maths harder than one who can say why a step works. Our guide to competency-based questions explains the question styles, and the Class 9 maths tuition guide covers the warning signs.

What the new Science book changes

NCERT's Exploration has 13 chapters, beginning with an introduction to secondary science. CBSE's 2026-27 course structure groups the exam chapters into four units, with the biology-based World of Living unit carrying the most marks (27 of 80). The marks by unit are in our syllabus guide. At home, the most useful habit is to connect each chapter with an experiment or an everyday example, and to ask your child to explain it in their own words.

The third language

R3 is compulsory in Class 9 from 2026-27. In the transitional phase, Class 9 students study the Class 6 level R3 textbook plus one local literary text, and the school assesses it, with the marksheet showing Qualified or Not Qualified. It should not take much time, but it does need to be passed. Details are in our three-language policy guide.

Eight things to do this term

  1. Check the books. Confirm that your child has Ganita Manjari Part I and Part II, Exploration, Kaveri and both Social Science parts.
  2. Ask for the annual plan. You want to know when each chapter is taught, when tests happen and which chapter list the school follows.
  3. Put internal assessment on the calendar. Twenty marks per subject come from tests, projects and a portfolio. Our guide to how the 20 internal marks are earned shows how to protect them.
  4. Practise explaining. After each Maths or Science chapter, ask your child to explain one idea out loud, as if teaching you.
  5. Do not ignore the asterisk questions, but do not stress about them. They are not examined. Treat them as stretch problems for good days.
  6. Use sample papers. CBSE releases sample papers and marking schemes on cbseacademic.nic.in. They show what application-style questions look like.
  7. Keep R3 light and regular. A few short sessions a week of reading aloud and conversation fit the design.
  8. Notice early. If marks slip, read our guide to improving Class 9 marks before adding more hours.

If your child is in Class 8 now

Class 9 will begin with coordinates and number systems, so practise plotting points and working with fractions and decimals. Make algebra and proportional reasoning from Class 8 solid, because Class 9 builds on them, and keep up a steady habit of explaining answers aloud. Our Class 8 syllabus guide and why Class 9 matters for the boards show what comes next.

Questions to ask your child's school

  • Which textbooks are being used, and are both parts of Maths and Social Science covered this year?
  • Which chapter list does the school follow for tests, CBSE's course structure or the textbook's chapter order?
  • Which language is R3, which book is used and how is it assessed?
  • What are the dates of the periodic assessments, the project and the portfolio?
  • How will the annual exam be structured, and will it follow the Class 10 board paper design?

Frequently asked questions

What is new in CBSE Class 9 for 2026-27?

New NCERT textbooks (Ganita Manjari for Maths, Exploration for Science and Kaveri for English), Maths chapters that earlier Class 9 books did not have, such as Sequences and Progressions, a compulsory third language, and application-style questions in line with CBSE's competency-focused papers.

Are the old Class 9 guides and notes still useful?

Only partly. Many ideas carry over, but chapter names, order and some content have changed, so notes and guides written for the older books will not match what your child is being taught.

Do asterisk questions in the Class 9 Maths book come in exams?

No. NCERT says questions and sections marked with an asterisk are for enrichment and are not to be assessed or included in examinations.

Is Class 9 Maths harder this year?

It places more emphasis on reasoning and explanation, and includes topics such as geometric progressions, which earlier Class 9 books did not. Children who understand why methods work usually cope well. Those who rely on memorised steps may find it harder, so start with explaining ideas aloud.

Do Class 9 marks count in the Class 10 board result?

The Class 10 board examination covers the Class 10 syllabus, and Class 9 is assessed by the school. Class 9 still matters because Class 10 builds on its concepts, which is why gaps are worth fixing now.

Does my child need tuition for the new Class 9 syllabus?

Not necessarily. Many children manage with steady home support and good school teaching. Tuition helps when there is a specific gap, such as proofs in geometry or algebra, or when a child has stopped asking questions. A demo class can help you judge.
