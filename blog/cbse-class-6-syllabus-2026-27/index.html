CBSE Class 6 Syllabus 2026-27: Every Chapter in Maths, Science, Social Science and English

Class 6 is the first year of middle school, and for many children it is the year when school suddenly feels bigger: separate subject books, more chapters, longer answers and, from this session, a compulsory third language. CBSE schools follow NCERT's textbooks, and the Class 6 books are a new generation of NCERT titles, so notes from an older sibling or a guide bought years ago may not match. This guide lists every chapter in the four core books for 2026-27, in NCERT's own words where possible, and shows where children usually need the most support.

The short answer

Class 6 CBSE students use four core NCERT books: Ganita Prakash for Maths (10 chapters), Curiosity for Science (12 chapters), Exploring Society: India and Beyond for Social Science (14 chapters) and Poorvi for English (5 units). The 2026-27 print run of each is a reprint of a new-generation book. On top of the core subjects, a third language (R3) is now compulsory in Class 6. Your school decides the order of chapters and the exam schedule, so ask for the annual plan.

What is different about Class 6 now

  • The books are new-generation NCERT titles. The Class 6 Maths and Science books were first published in 2024 and are reprinted for 2026-27. Chapter names and order differ from the older Class 6 books, so old guides, worksheets and notes will not map one-to-one.
  • A third language is compulsory. CBSE's 2026-27 curriculum makes R3 compulsory from Class 6, assessed by the school. See our guide to the CBSE three-language policy.
  • Learning is meant to be activity-based. NCERT says the Science book is built around activities and reflective questions and aims at experience-based learning rather than reading and memorising. The Maths book likewise stresses patterns, puzzles and connections with other subjects. Tests in this style tend to ask children to apply ideas, which we explain in our guide to competency-based questions.

You can see the official textbooks on NCERT's textbook page. The chapter lists below are taken from the contents pages of the 2026-27 editions.

Maths: Ganita Prakash (10 chapters)

NCERT's book begins with a chapter on patterns contributed by the mathematician Manjul Bhargava and then moves through different areas of mathematics. Here is each chapter with NCERT's own description of what it covers, put in simple words.

ChapterWhat NCERT says it covers
1. Patterns in MathematicsMathematics as a search for patterns: sequences of numbers and shapes. These patterns are revisited in later chapters.
2. Lines and AnglesThe building blocks of geometry: points, line segments, rays, lines and angles, and how to measure angles.
3. Number PlayGames and puzzles with numbers, some of them still unsolved.
4. Data Handling and PresentationCollecting and presenting data, including its analytic and aesthetic sides.
5. Prime TimePrime numbers as the building blocks of whole numbers, and factorisation.
6. Perimeter and AreaA revision of these ideas with challenging puzzles.
7. FractionsBuilding intuition for fractions gradually, starting from fractional units such as 1/10, then comparing, adding and subtracting fractions.
8. Playing with ConstructionsHands-on drawing of shapes using a compass and a ruler.
9. SymmetryAn artistic, hands-on look at symmetry.
10. The Other Side of ZeroBuilding intuition for negative numbers, then the rules for adding and subtracting all integers.

Source: contents and 'About the Book' pages of NCERT's Ganita Prakash for Grade 6 (reprint 2026-27), summarised in simple words.

Where children often need extra support: NCERT describes Fractions as many students' first encounter with the idea, and the Other Side of Zero introduces negative numbers. Prime Time and Playing with Constructions add skills that are new at this level. These are the chapters where it helps to practise a little every day instead of the week before a test. Science: Curiosity (12 chapters)

NCERT says science in the middle stage takes an integrated approach, building basic capacities across biology, chemistry, physics and earth science. The first chapter is an introduction to how science works and, by NCERT's design, has no assessment exercises.

Because the book is activity-based, children who enjoy doing and asking questions often do well, and children who learn only by memorising can find tests harder. A simple habit helps: after each chapter, ask your child to explain one activity from it to you, including why it worked.

Class 6 Social Science brings geography, history, civics and economics ideas together in one book, so children can need help seeing how the themes connect. Maps, timelines and short written answers are the main skills to practise. See our guide to social studies study tips.

The book is built around stories, poems and short non-fiction. The best help at home is reading aloud, talking about what happened and why, and writing a few sentences about each lesson. Our guides on reading comprehension and English writing give simple routines.

The third language (R3) in Class 6

CBSE's curriculum says R3 is compulsory from Class 6 from the 2026-27 session, that it is designed for two or three periods a week, and that the focus is on oral and reading fluency, not heavy grammar or writing. It is assessed by the school and is a qualifying subject. Schools choose the language, within CBSE's rules, so ask yours which language it is and which book it uses.

How to plan the year

  1. Ask for the annual plan. Schools set the order of chapters and the dates of the half-yearly and annual exams, so this tells you what is coming and when.
  2. Spread revision out. Ten chapters in Maths and twelve in Science are manageable only if a chapter is revised soon after it is taught, not saved for the exam. A short recall session once a week is enough.
  3. Keep a doubt list. In a bigger class, children can stop asking questions. A notebook page of doubts, taken to school or to a tutor, prevents small confusions from piling up.
  4. Protect sleep and routine. The jump to Class 6 is tiring. Our guide on why marks drop in Classes 6 to 8 explains the common causes and a two-week plan.
  5. Look ahead. Class 6 sets up the next two years, and by Class 8 the books become heavier. See our Class 8 syllabus guide to see where this leads, and the Class 7 guide for the year in between.

Frequently asked questions

Which books does CBSE Class 6 use in 2026-27?

CBSE schools follow NCERT textbooks. The core books are Ganita Prakash for Maths, Curiosity for Science, Exploring Society: India and Beyond for Social Science and Poorvi for English, plus language books chosen by the school, including for the third language.

How many chapters are there in Class 6 Maths and Science?

Ganita Prakash, the Class 6 Maths book, has 10 chapters. Curiosity, the Class 6 Science book, has 12 chapters. The Social Science book has 14 chapters across five themes, and the English book has 5 units.

Is the Class 6 syllabus new?

The Class 6 books are new-generation NCERT titles first published in 2024 for Maths and Science, aligned to the National Curriculum Framework for School Education 2023. They differ from older Class 6 books, so use current books and avoid old notes.

Is a third language compulsory in Class 6?

Yes. CBSE's 2026-27 curriculum makes a third language (R3) compulsory from Class 6. It is assessed by the school, designed for two or three periods a week, and focuses on speaking and reading fluency.

Where can I download the Class 6 NCERT books?

NCERT publishes its textbooks as free PDFs on its official website, ncert.nic.in, under the textbook section. Use the Grade 6 books for the 2026-27 session.

Which Class 6 chapters do children usually find hardest?

It varies by child, but the chapters that introduce new ideas are the likeliest to need extra practice. In Maths those include Fractions and The Other Side of Zero, which introduces negative numbers, plus Prime Time and Playing with Constructions. In Science the activity-based style can be harder for children who only memorise.
