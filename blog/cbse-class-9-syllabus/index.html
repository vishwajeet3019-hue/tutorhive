Class 9 · CBSE syllabus 2026-27

CBSE Class 9 Syllabus 2026-27: Maths, Science, Social Science, English

Class 9 has no board exam, so it is easy to treat it as a year to coast through. That would be a mistake. A large part of what makes Class 10 boards hard is content that was first introduced in Class 9 - polynomials, linear equations, geometry proofs, atomic structure, motion. This guide lays out the full CBSE Class 9 syllabus chapter by chapter across all four core subjects, so parents and students know exactly what the year covers and why it matters more than the absence of an exam suggests.

Why Class 9 is worth taking seriously

Class 9 marks do not appear on a board certificate and are rarely asked about outside the school. That makes it tempting to treat Class 9 as a breather year between Class 8 and the "real" pressure of Class 10 boards. In practice, the opposite is closer to the truth: Class 9 is where a student either builds the foundation that makes Class 10 manageable, or accumulates the gaps that make Class 10 feel like a scramble.

Chapters like Polynomials, Linear Equations, Triangles and Circles in Maths, or atomic structure and motion in Science, are not repeated from scratch in Class 10 - they are extended and applied in harder, board-style questions. A student who understood these properly in Class 9 spends Class 10 practising and refining. A student who memorised through Class 9 without real understanding has to relearn the basics under board-exam pressure, with far less time to do it in.

CBSE Class 9 Maths syllabus

Class 9 Maths is organised into five broad areas: Number Systems, Algebra, Coordinate Geometry, Geometry, and Mensuration, with Statistics and Probability introduced toward the end of the year.

Number Systems. Rational and irrational numbers, representing numbers on the number line, and the laws of exponents for real numbers. This chapter sets up notation used across the rest of the year.

Polynomials. Definitions, degree, zeroes of a polynomial, the remainder and factor theorems, and algebraic identities used to factorise expressions. This is one of the most important chapters in the entire syllabus - Class 10's polynomial and quadratic-equation work assumes this is solid.

Coordinate Geometry. The Cartesian plane, plotting points, and reading coordinates - a short chapter that is foundational for Class 10 Coordinate Geometry.

Linear Equations in Two Variables. Writing and graphing linear equations, and solutions of a linear equation. This leads directly into Class 10's Pair of Linear Equations chapter.

Introduction to Euclid's Geometry. Euclid's axioms and postulates - a conceptual chapter that builds the logical basis for proof-writing used throughout the geometry chapters that follow.

Lines and Angles. Angle pairs formed by intersecting and parallel lines, and the angle sum property of a triangle, proved using these results.

Triangles. Congruence rules (SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS) and properties of triangles, proved through formal two-column reasoning. This proof-writing skill carries forward directly into Class 10's Triangles and Circles chapters.

Quadrilaterals. Properties of parallelograms and the mid-point theorem.

Circles. Angle subtended by a chord, and the relationship between equal chords and equal angles - groundwork for the tangent-focused Circles chapter in Class 10.

Heron's Formula. Finding the area of a triangle when the three sides are known, without needing the height.

Surface Areas and Volumes. Surface area and volume of cubes, cuboids, cylinders, cones, and spheres - the direct precursor to Class 10's combination-of-solids chapter.

Statistics. Collection and graphical representation of data (bar graphs, histograms), and mean, median, mode of ungrouped data.

Probability. An empirical, experiment-based introduction to probability, which becomes classical (theoretical) probability in Class 10.

CBSE Class 9 Science syllabus

Class 9 Science is taught in three sections - Physics, Chemistry, and Biology - each contributing roughly equal weight across the year.

Physics:

Motion - distance and displacement, speed and velocity, acceleration, and graphical representation of motion.
Force and Laws of Motion - Newton's three laws, inertia, momentum, and conservation of momentum.
Gravitation - the universal law of gravitation, free fall, weight, and thrust and pressure.
Work and Energy - work done by a force, kinetic and potential energy, and the law of conservation of energy.
Sound - production and propagation of sound, characteristics of sound waves, and the human ear.

Chemistry:

Matter in Our Surroundings - states of matter and changes of state, evaporation.
Is Matter Around Us Pure - mixtures, solutions, colloids, suspensions, and physical/chemical changes.
Atoms and Molecules - laws of chemical combination, atomic and molecular mass, mole concept, and writing chemical formulae.
Structure of the Atom - discovery of the electron, proton, and neutron; Bohr's model; valency; isotopes and isobars.

Biology:

The Fundamental Unit of Life - cell structure, cell organelles, and the differences between plant and animal cells.
Tissues - plant and animal tissue types and their functions.
Improvement in Food Resources - crop production and management, and animal husbandry.

The exact biology chapter list has shifted with rationalisation in recent years, so confirm the current set with your child's school. The physics and chemistry core listed above - motion, force, atomic structure, and the mole concept - has stayed consistent and is the part that Class 10 Science builds on most directly.

CBSE Class 9 Social Science syllabus

Social Science is taught across four books, one for each discipline.

History - India and the Contemporary World I: the French Revolution, socialism and the Russian Revolution, Nazism and the rise of Hitler, forest society and colonialism, and pastoralists in the modern world.

Geography - Contemporary India I: India's size and location, physical features, drainage, climate, natural vegetation and wildlife, and population.

Political Science - Democratic Politics I: what democracy is and why it matters, constitutional design, electoral politics, the working of institutions, and democratic rights.

Economics: the story of a village economy, people as a resource, poverty as a challenge, and food security in India.

Unlike Maths and Science, Social Science content itself does not repeat directly in Class 10 - the two years cover different History, Geography, Political Science, and Economics themes. What does carry forward is the skill: writing structured, point-wise answers, reading source-based extracts carefully, and doing accurate map work. A student who builds this habit in Class 9 walks into Class 10 Social Science already knowing how to answer, not just what to answer.

CBSE Class 9 English syllabus

English is built around two core NCERT texts - a main course book covering prose and poetry, and a supplementary reader of shorter stories - alongside grammar, writing skills (letters, stories, diary entries), and reading comprehension. The specific chapter titles can vary slightly by school and edition, but the skill focus is consistent: comprehension of unseen passages, structured writing to a format and word limit, and grammar accuracy. These are the same skills the Class 10 English paper tests, at a slightly higher difficulty, so steady practice in Class 9 - not just finishing the chapters - pays off directly the following year.

How this syllabus sets up Class 10 boards

The connection between Class 9 and Class 10 is strongest in Maths and Science, where entire Class 10 chapters are direct extensions of Class 9 ones:

Polynomials (Class 9) → Polynomials and Quadratic Equations (Class 10)
Linear Equations in Two Variables (Class 9) → Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables (Class 10)
Triangles, Circles (Class 9) → Triangles, Circles with tangents (Class 10)
Surface Areas and Volumes of single solids (Class 9) → combinations of solids (Class 10)
Structure of the Atom, Atoms and Molecules (Class 9) → Chemical Reactions, Periodic Classification (Class 10)
Motion, Force and Laws of Motion (Class 9) → Electricity, Light (Class 10, applying the same problem-solving approach)

A student who is weak in the Class 9 version of these topics does not get a fresh start in Class 10 - the gap simply resurfaces, now inside a board-marked question, with far less time to fix it. This is the real argument for taking Class 9 seriously: not the exam itself, but what it quietly determines about how Class 10 feels. A realistic way to plan the Class 9 year

Because there is no external exam, Class 9 is the lowest-pressure year available to fix weak foundations properly - a genuine advantage if it is used well.

April/June to December: move through chapters roughly alongside the school's pace, but do not just finish a chapter and move on - make sure each one is actually understood, especially Polynomials, Linear Equations, and the geometry proof chapters in Maths, and the Physics and Chemistry fundamentals in Science.
Through the year: keep up with periodic tests and treat them as real feedback, not just formality - a pattern of dropping marks in the same topic across two or three tests is a signal worth acting on early rather than at the end of the year.
Before the annual exam: consolidate with previous years' school papers and revise the full syllabus, the same way Class 10 students revise for boards - this builds the habit before it becomes high-stakes.

Frequently asked questions

How many subjects and chapters are there in CBSE Class 9?

The core academic subjects are Maths, Science, Social Science, and English, each with roughly 10-15 chapters or units depending on the subject and the year's rationalisation. Maths has around 12-13 chapters across number systems, algebra, geometry, mensuration, and statistics/probability. Science splits into Physics, Chemistry, and Biology sections. Social Science covers History, Geography, Political Science, and Economics. Always confirm the exact current-year chapter list against the official CBSE circular or your child's school, since NCERT periodically revises and rationalises chapters.

Does Class 9 have board exams?

No. Class 9 exams are conducted by the school, not by CBSE, and typically follow a periodic test plus term/annual exam structure similar to earlier classes. There is no external board exam in Class 9. The academic weight of Class 9 comes not from an exam but from the fact that a large share of the Class 10 board syllabus builds directly on Class 9 chapters.

Which Class 9 chapters matter most for Class 10 boards?

In Maths: Polynomials, Linear Equations in Two Variables, and the geometry proof chapters (Triangles, Circles) are the foundation for Class 10 Algebra and Geometry. In Science: atomic structure and the mole concept in Chemistry, and motion and force in Physics, are extended directly in Class 10. In Social Science, the way of writing structured, point-wise answers matters more than any single chapter carrying forward. A student who is shaky in these areas going into Class 10 usually struggles more than one who is confident in them.

How should a Class 9 student plan the academic year?

A steady pace through the school year - roughly matching the school's own chapter order from April/June through December, followed by consolidation and previous test papers before the final exams - works better than last-minute cramming. Because Class 9 has no external stakes, it is also the lowest-pressure year in which to fix weak foundations properly, before Class 10 adds board-exam pressure on top of the same content.
