CBSE Three-Language Policy: What R1, R2 and R3 Mean for Your Child

If your child is in Class 6 or Class 9 this year, you may have heard a new set of letters at school: R1, R2 and R3. CBSE's 2026-27 curriculum introduces a three-language framework and makes a third language compulsory from Class 6 and Class 9. It is one of the biggest structural changes in recent years, and the details are easy to get wrong when they are passed on by word of mouth. Here is what the curriculum document actually says, in plain language, with the parts that matter most to parents.

The short answer

CBSE now organises languages as R1, R2 and R3, and from the 2026-27 session a third language (R3) is compulsory in Class 6 and Class 9. At least two of a student's three languages must be native Indian languages. R3 is assessed by the school, not by a board exam, and it is a qualifying subject: CBSE says the marksheet will show Qualified or Not Qualified. It is designed to take two or three periods a week, with a focus on speaking and reading fluency rather than heavy grammar or writing.

What R1, R2 and R3 mean

CBSE's curriculum organises language study through a three-language framework across school stages, named R1, R2 and R3. Under the framework, two of the three languages must be native to India, in line with the National Curriculum Framework for School Education 2023.

  • R1: any language from the list CBSE offers.
  • R2: a language other than the one chosen as R1.
  • R3: a language other than the ones chosen as R1 and R2.

CBSE gives these examples of valid combinations: Hindi (R1), English (R2) and Malayalam (R3); English (R1), Bengali (R2) and Hindi (R3); and Marathi (R1), Kannada (R2) and English (R3). One language cannot be taken at more than one level. Which languages are offered at each level is decided by each school, within CBSE's rules.

When it applies to your child

CBSE says R3 shall be made compulsory from Class 6 and Class 9 with effect from the 2026-27 session. For the first batches, the curriculum describes a transitional phase.

  • 2026-27: R3 is compulsory in Class 6 and, as a transitional arrangement, in Class 9. Class 9 students study the Class 6 level R3 textbooks published or prescribed by NCERT or CBSE, plus one local literary text.
  • 2027-28: the same Class 9 students, now in Class 10, study the Class 7 level R3 textbook plus one local literary text. R3 continues year by year for the Class 6 batch as well.
  • 2028-29 onwards: R3 is compulsory across the stages in its full form.

CBSE also notes that for Class 10 from the 2028 examination onwards, R1 and R2 will have separate syllabus coverage and assessment frameworks, even where the same textbook is used. That change does not affect students in the current batches, but it is why you may hear the terms discussed together.

How R3 is assessed, and whether it counts

This is the part parents worry about most, and the curriculum is quite specific.

  • It is assessed by the school. R3 assessment is entirely school-based or internal, and there is no separate board examination in R3 at the Class 10 level.
  • It is a qualifying subject. CBSE says R3 will remain a mandatory qualifying subject, and the marksheet will distinctly reflect "Qualified" or "Not Qualified". Schools are encouraged to award internal grades from A to E and to include qualitative feedback in the Holistic Progress Card.
  • Passing it is required. Successful completion of R3 is mandatory for the award of CBSE certification, and no student is eligible for the Secondary School Examination Pass Certificate without passing the third language.
  • There is a second chance. Students who do not qualify in Class 10 despite appearing in the board examination are re-assessed by their school at a suitable time and the result is conveyed to CBSE. Not clearing R3 at the time of admit card distribution does not stop a student from appearing in the board examination in other subjects.

In short: R3 does not add a board paper. It does need to be passed, so it should be taken seriously rather than ignored. Rules around results can be refined by CBSE, so check your school and cbse.gov.in for circulars.

How much work is R3 meant to be?

CBSE addresses this directly. The R3 course is designed for two or three periods a week, which the board says can be accommodated within the existing weekly framework. The focus is on oral and reading fluency, not heavy grammar or writing. That is a useful guide to how much time and pressure R3 should take at home: a little regular practice, not an extra heavy subject.

Textbooks follow the same phased approach. CBSE said Class 6 R3 books for 2026-27 would be available by the end of June 2026, and that Class 9 R3 books for the 2027-28 batch would be available by December 2026. If your child's school has not given you the R3 book yet, ask which one they are using.

Which languages, and who is exempt

  • School choice. Schools may choose any language from CBSE's list of subjects, provided at least two of R1, R2 and R3 are native Indian languages. A foreign language can be offered as part of the three-language formula alongside two native Indian languages, or as a fourth additional language. Schools had to update their R3 offering on CBSE's OASIS portal by 30 June 2026.
  • If your child has not studied a native Indian language. A footnote in the curriculum says that students who have not studied even one native Indian language in Classes 6 to 8 must take a native Indian language at R3.
  • Children with special educational needs. Children with certified specific learning disabilities or other specified conditions may be exempted from R2 and R3, as per the Examination Bye Laws and the RPwD Act, 2016.
  • Students from abroad. Students joining Class 9 in 2026-27 from foreign boards, or from CBSE schools abroad, who have not studied a native Indian language, may be exempted case by case. CBSE schools located in foreign countries are exempt from the two-native-languages requirement.

What parents of Class 6 and Class 9 should do now

  1. Find out which language is R3. Ask the school which language it offers, which book it uses and how many periods a week are allotted.
  2. Ask how it is assessed. Ask for the internal assessment plan and the sample rubric. CBSE said it would provide a sample question paper and rubric for the Class 9 transitional phase.
  3. Keep it light and regular. A few short sessions a week of reading aloud and conversation fit the design far better than occasional cramming.
  4. Use home languages as an advantage. If the R3 language is spoken at home or by grandparents, daily conversation is the best practice there is.
  5. Protect the core subjects. R3 should not push out the time your child needs for maths, science, English and social science, which still carry the board marks. If marks in those subjects are slipping, see why marks drop in Class 6, 7 and 8.

Frequently asked questions

What are R1, R2 and R3 in CBSE?

They are the three language levels in CBSE's three-language framework. R1 is any language CBSE offers, R2 is a different language from R1, and R3 is a language different from both R1 and R2. At least two of the three languages must be native to India.

From which class and year is R3 compulsory in CBSE?

CBSE says R3 is compulsory from Class 6 and Class 9 from the 2026-27 session. Students in Class 9 in 2026-27 study the Class 6 level R3 textbook plus one local literary text, and the same students study the Class 7 level textbook plus a local literary text in Class 10 in 2027-28. From 2028-29 onwards R3 is compulsory in its full form across the stages.

Is there a board exam for the third language in Class 10?

No. CBSE says R3 assessment is school-based and there is no separate board examination in R3 at the Class 10 level. It is a mandatory qualifying subject, and the marksheet will show Qualified or Not Qualified.

What happens if my child does not qualify in R3?

Students who do not qualify in Class 10 are re-assessed by their school at a suitable time and the school conveys the result to CBSE. Not clearing R3 at admit card distribution does not stop a student from appearing in the board exam in other subjects, but a student cannot receive the Secondary School Examination Pass Certificate without passing the third language.

How many hours a week is the third language?

CBSE says the R3 course is designed for two or three periods per week, within the existing weekly framework, with the focus on oral and reading fluency rather than heavy grammar or writing.

Can my child be exempted from the third language?

CBSE describes limited exemptions. Children with certified specific learning disabilities or other specified conditions may be exempted from R2 and R3, as per the Examination Bye Laws. Students joining Class 9 in 2026-27 from foreign boards or CBSE schools abroad who have not studied a native Indian language may be exempted on a case-by-case basis.
