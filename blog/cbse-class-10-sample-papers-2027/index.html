CBSE Class 10 Sample Papers 2027: A Subject-Wise Practice Plan That Actually Raises Marks

Most Class 10 students download a stack of sample papers and then use them badly: reading through them instead of writing, skipping the timer, marking themselves kindly, and doing eight Maths papers but no Social Science. Done properly, the official CBSE sample paper and a handful of previous-year papers are the single most reliable way to lift board marks in the last few months. This is where to get the right papers, how the marking scheme works, how to sit one like the real thing, and a three-phase plan built around the February–March 2027 exam.

What a CBSE sample paper actually is

A CBSE sample question paper is the Board's own model of the real exam for that year. It is released by CBSE, matches the current syllabus, and reflects the difficulty level, the question types and the section-wise structure the Board intends to set. Alongside each sample paper, CBSE releases a marking scheme: the expected answers, the step-wise breakdown of marks, and the value points an examiner looks for.

It is not a leaked paper, and it is not a prediction of the exact questions. It is a blueprint. Treat it as the reference standard that every other paper you solve should be checked against.

Where to get the official papers

The only official source is cbseacademic.nic.in, under the Sample Question Papers section for the current session. For each subject you will find two PDFs: the question paper and the marking scheme. CBSE usually publishes these early in the session, ahead of the board exams, so check the site from the start of the academic year and download the moment they appear.

Be careful with everything else. Printed guides and random websites often carry papers labelled "CBSE sample paper" that follow an old pattern, an outdated syllabus, or a marking style the Board no longer uses. Previous-year board papers and well-reviewed publisher practice sets are useful for volume, but the official sample paper on cbseacademic.nic.in is the one that defines the target for your year. When something in a practice paper looks unfamiliar, the official sample paper and its marking scheme settle it.

The marking scheme you are practising against

Before you solve anything, be clear about what the paper is out of and how it is scored. This has not changed for 2027.

Each main subject is out of 100 — an 80-mark theory paper of three hours, plus 20 marks of internal assessment marked by your school.
You need 33% overall in a subject to pass it.
There is normally a 15-minute reading window before writing time begins; use it to read the whole paper and mark the questions you will attempt first.
In recent sample papers, roughly half the theory paper is competency-based — case studies, source-based sets, assertion–reason and application questions — about a fifth is straight objective or MCQ type, and the rest is short and long written answers.

Always cross-check that split against the official CBSE sample paper for your exam year on cbseacademic.nic.in, since the Board adjusts it from time to time. For the full context on the two-exam calendar, see our explainer on the CBSE Class 10 two board exams in 2027 — the short version is that both sittings use the same syllabus and pattern, so you practise once and aim to peak for the compulsory February–March paper.

How to actually sit a sample paper

A paper you read is worth almost nothing. A paper you sit under real conditions and then mark honestly is worth more than a week of passive revision. The routine:

One sitting, full three hours, in the morning if you can, at the same start time as the real paper.
No notes, no textbook, no phone. Only the question paper, blank ruled sheets, and your geometry box or calculator where the subject allows it.
Write the whole thing out — full steps, full answers, diagrams, the lot. Answer-writing stamina is a skill and it only builds by writing.
Then self-mark strictly against the official marking scheme, the same day. Give yourself only the marks an examiner would give, not the marks you feel you deserve.
Keep an error log. For every mark lost, write one line: which chapter, and what kind of mistake — concept not known, silly calculation slip, question misread, ran out of time, answer too short for the word limit, diagram unlabelled. Patterns show up fast, and the log becomes your revision list.

A three-phase plan around the February–March 2027 exam

This maps onto a normal Class 10 year and works alongside a realistic daily study timetable and the month-by-month board exam preparation timeline.

Phase 1 — now to about November: chapter-wise, not full papers. As you finish revising each chapter, pull just that chapter's questions from past sample papers and previous-year papers and solve those. You may keep the book open early on. The aim is to see how the Board frames questions on that specific topic and to fix gaps while the chapter is fresh. Use the chapter lists for Maths, Science and Social Science to track what has been covered.

Phase 2 — December to January: full papers under exam conditions. Once a subject's syllabus has had one full revision pass, start sitting complete three-hour papers — about one paper per subject per week, rotated so no subject is left out. Begin with the official CBSE sample paper, then move to previous-year and quality practice papers. Self-evaluate every single one and update the error log.

Phase 3 — the last 15 to 20 days: alternate paper and repair. One day a fresh full paper, the next day working only through your error log and the marking schemes of papers already done. No new topics in this window. The goal is to stop repeating the same three or four mistakes that are quietly costing 8–12 marks a paper.

Subject-wise notes

Mathematics. Marks here are step marks. Write every step, show LHS and RHS, carry units, and never skip the "to prove" and "given" lines in geometry. In constructions and graphs, accuracy and labelling carry marks on their own. When you self-mark, notice whether losses are concept gaps or avoidable slips — the fix is different for each. Our guide on Class 10 Maths board preparation covers the high-weightage chapters and the mistakes that cost the most.

Science. Diagrams must be neat and fully labelled, chemical equations balanced with state symbols, and definitions written close to the textbook wording. For numericals, always show the formula, the substitution and the final unit. Source-based and assertion–reason questions reward careful reading more than extra knowledge. See Class 9 and 10 Science tuition for where Physics, Chemistry and Biology marks usually leak.

Social Science. Map work is free marks if practised and lost entirely if not. Answers are keyword-driven — examiners scan for the value points in the marking scheme — so structure answers in points and respect the word limits. Practise source-based questions with the passage covered first, then revealed. Our Social Science syllabus guide shows where History, Geography, Civics and Economics marks are commonly dropped.

English and the second language. Time the unseen passage and the writing section tightly — these are where students overrun and then rush the literature answers. Learn the exact formats and word counts for letter, notice, analytical paragraph and any other writing tasks, and lose no marks to format or grammar. In literature, revise the marking-scheme value points for each text rather than re-reading whole chapters.

Read the marking scheme like an examiner

Most students never open the marking scheme properly, and it is where the easy marks hide. It tells you exactly how a 3-mark or 5-mark answer is split, when "any two points" is enough, which value points must appear, and where step marks are awarded even if the final answer is wrong. Reading a few marking schemes closely teaches you to write answers that are aimed at the marks — the right length, the right structure, the key terms present — instead of long answers that still miss points. Common mistakes with sample papers

Reading papers instead of writing them out in full.
Solving without a timer, so speed is never tested until the real exam.
Self-marking generously, or not marking at all.
Doing many papers in a strong subject and none in a weak one.
Starting full papers before the syllabus is covered, then losing confidence over topics not yet studied.
Never keeping an error log, so the same mistakes repeat into February.
Ignoring the internal-assessment 20 marks — practicals, portfolio and periodic tests through the year are part of the final score too.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I get the official CBSE Class 10 sample papers for 2027?

The only official source is the CBSE Academic website, cbseacademic.nic.in, under the Sample Question Papers section. CBSE usually publishes the sample paper and its marking scheme for each subject early in the session, ahead of the board exams. Each subject has a downloadable question paper PDF and a separate marking scheme PDF. Papers sold in shops or on random websites labelled as CBSE sample papers are not official and often do not match the current pattern, so always cross-check against the PDF on cbseacademic.nic.in.

How is the CBSE Class 10 sample paper marked?

Each main subject is out of 100: an 80-mark theory paper of three hours plus 20 marks of internal assessment done by your school. You need 33% overall in a subject to pass. In recent sample papers roughly half the theory paper is competency-based, about a fifth is objective or MCQ type, and the rest is short and long written answers. Confirm the exact split from the official CBSE sample paper for your exam year, since the Board revises it from time to time.

How many sample papers should a Class 10 student solve before the boards?

There is no fixed number, but a workable target is the official CBSE sample paper plus five to eight full previous-year or practice papers per subject, sat under timed conditions, with every paper self-evaluated against the marking scheme. Quality matters far more than quantity. Ten papers solved carelessly and never checked help less than four papers sat properly and analysed for repeated mistakes.

When should I start solving full sample papers?

Start full three-hour papers only after the whole syllabus for that subject has been revised at least once, which for most students is around December to January before a February–March board exam. Before that, use sample papers chapter by chapter: after you revise a chapter, solve only that chapter's questions from past papers. Sitting a full paper on a half-covered syllabus mostly measures what you have not studied yet and dents confidence for no reason.

Are CBSE sample papers enough, or do I need previous year question papers too?

Use both. The official sample paper shows the current pattern and difficulty level the Board intends for your year. Previous year papers give you volume and show which concepts and question types repeat. A good mix is one official sample paper to understand the blueprint, then previous year papers and quality practice papers for repetition, all checked against CBSE marking schemes.

Does the twice-a-year Class 10 board exam from 2027 change how I use sample papers?

Not in terms of practice. The syllabus, paper structure and marking scheme are the same for both sittings, so you prepare and practise exactly as you would for a single board exam and aim to peak for the compulsory February–March paper. The second exam in May–June is optional and meant for improvement in a few subjects, so treat it as a backup, not a reason to practise less for the first one.
